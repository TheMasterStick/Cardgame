# World Bible — Working Foundation

This file separates **setting identity** from mechanical card Allegiance. It records what the world currently knows, while keeping unresolved ideas visibly unresolved instead of silently canonizing them.

## 1. Core taxonomy

- **Gameplay archetype** — deck/class fantasy such as Kingdom, Mage Order, Might Tribe, High Elves, Celestial Host. Not automatically a nation or political faction.
- **Mechanical Faction/Allegiance** — engine tag used to restrict deckbuilding when a Hero requires it.
- **Race** — what a being is.
- **CreatureType** — what combat role a creature performs on the board.
- **Culture / people** — shared customs and identity such as Cimbar, Skaldjborn or Lorthaine.
- **State / house / city / territory** — geopolitical entity such as a kingdom, march, city-state or noble house.
- **Organization** — company/order/guild that can cross political borders, such as the Golden Company.

One card can belong to several of these categories without those categories becoming engine tags.

## 2. Mortal racial baseline

The setting intentionally uses recognizable classic-fantasy racial blocs rather than a default cosmopolitan “every race in every city” model.

- Humans, Dwarves, Gnomes, Elves and other traditionally graceful/civilized peoples generally tolerate one another well enough for diplomacy, trade and occasional shared ventures. Their settlements still normally remain culturally/racially distinct.
- Orcs, Goblins, Undead, Dark Elves and similar peoples occupy a more hostile/distrusted bloc with many local exceptions.
- A Goblin merchant can sometimes be tolerated in a Human market while still facing severe prejudice and scrutiny.
- A particularly formidable, ruthless or culturally compatible Human may earn grudging acceptance among Orcs or even certain Undead powers. Exceptions are noteworthy precisely because the normal boundaries remain real.
- Mixed-race organizations should exist only when there is a convincing in-world reason: frontier necessity, ancient alliance, trade mission, religion, catastrophe, exile, mercenary policy, demonic corruption, etc. Never mix species merely to tick a roster box.

### Angels and Demons

Angels and Demons sit partly outside ordinary mortal racial politics. Mortals become entangled in their conflicts, but the celestial/infernal powers pursue their own metaphysical agendas. Demons in particular do not care about mortal race when tempting, bargaining with or corrupting someone; desire, ambition and weakness matter more than ancestry.

## 3. Confirmed/working mortal identities

### Cimbar — culture

A secluded, isolationist Human warrior culture inspired by the feeling of Conan/Cimmerian barbarism without being a direct copy. Cimbar are not famous for refinement or eloquence. Their global reputation comes from terrifying individual martial capability: stories of Cimbar breaking forces at roughly ten-to-one odds are believable enough that commanders treat them seriously. Individual clans/people are usually scattered and insular; if and when Cimbar unite, they are regarded as one of the most fearsome peoples on the planet.

**Card anchor:** Cimbar Berserker — male; Frenzy mechanically represents escalating offensive momentum with each attack.

### Lorthaine — culture

Lorthaine is primarily a **martial culture/tradition**, not a deck faction. Its swordsmasters/duelists are renowned abroad in the same social sense that a Braavosi swordsman has an instantly recognizable reputation in another fantasy setting. The exact homeland/political form remains open.

**Card anchor:** Lorthaine Elite Veteran — Duel supports the culture's reputation for selecting and defeating worthy opponents.

### Skaldjborn — culture

Northern Norse/Viking-inspired Human people. Existing Neutral cards establish the name and martial visual identity; deeper politics/geography remain open.

### Klamet / Kleh'meht — place + ancient history

Klamet is a desert/Arabian-inspired settlement known for pit fighting. Working historical layer: the ancient ruler/founder **Kleh'meht** predates the modern name, with linguistic drift toward Klamet over centuries. This permits modern pit-fighting Klamet and an older royal/archaeological tradition to coexist without renaming the existing card.

**Card anchor:** Pit Fighter of Klamet.
**Future hook:** Jewel of Kleh'meht and other dispersed royal relics.

## 4. Gestmane — deliberately unresolved

The Warhorn of Gestmane card establishes a massive horn-and-bell tower decorated with both Red and Blue banners while those forces stand peacefully around it. Gestmane itself is not locked yet. Strong compatible possibilities include:

- **House Gestmane** — an ancient family whose great horn symbolizes its authority;
- **Gestmane as place/territory** — fortress, march or old realm named for/ruled by that house;
- **a third political power** between or adjacent to the Red and Blue powers;
- **the demonic frontier** — Gestmane may be the farthest mortal power on the edge of a demonic wasteland, maintaining that border for centuries;
- **the Great Summons** — sounding the Warhorn may invoke an old obligation that causes even rival Red/Blue powers to send forces.

These ideas are compatible: House Gestmane could rule the Gestmane March on the demonic frontier, while the Great Warhorn is both their dynastic symbol and the continental alarm that summons neighboring powers. This combined form remains a strong proposal, not yet locked canon.

## 5. The Red and Blue powers — unnamed

Recurring AI card art has repeatedly produced major Red and Blue heraldic/military identities. Recruitment Station shows the colors together; Warhorn of Gestmane shows both peacefully present.

Current rule: **do not name, merge, or place them too close geographically yet.** They may be allies, rivals, two major continental players, political spheres, or powers bound by old obligations such as Gestmane's defense. Let more art accumulate before fixing borders.

## 6. Organizations

### Golden Company — confirmed core, details still developing

A highly prized mercenary company. The name remains despite similar fantasy precedents; its final identity should be differentiated through setting-specific visual/history/mechanical traits rather than simply copying the famous “elite honorable expensive sellswords” template.

Working direction from the existing Captain art: dark professional armor with accumulated gilding and an assault/shock role. A possible custom is that successful service literally adds gold/gilded fittings to veterans' armor, making seniority and completed contracts visible. Treat this as a strong proposal until more cards lock it in.

### Flankguard / unicorn cavalry — working organization

Human cavalry tradition/order associated with unicorn mounts and flank warfare. The user-established direction includes some tie to Elves without being a formal alliance: tolerated/recognized rather than fully integrated. Exact name, pact and political home remain open.

**Card anchor:** Flankguard Outrider.

## 7. Mixed adventuring bands

Mixed-race groups are unusual enough to be story-worthy. A planned Neutral Ability, **Band of Merry Folk**, intentionally plays with the classic fellowship/adventuring-party fantasy: two Human warriors, a Human mage, a Dwarf engineer, an Elf ranger and three Gnomes. Its art/mechanic is on the back burner until more non-Human cards exist.

When racial synergy becomes mechanically relevant, the card is a natural candidate to reward controlling several different Races.

## 8. Non-Human worldbuilding policy

The race pool itself is already selected, with a deliberate bias toward recognizable fantasy peoples that also have strong adult-fantasy/fan-service visual potential. The next worldbuilding work is not “which races exist?” but **where and how they exist**:

- establish homelands/cultures only as cards or setting needs demand;
- preserve strong classic-fantasy silhouettes/tropes so a card immediately reads as Dwarf, Elf, Orc, Goblin, etc.;
- then differentiate cultures inside each race rather than treating a whole species as one state;
- keep racial mingling meaningful and contextual, not universal background noise.

## 9. Canon-status convention

Use these labels when expanding this file:

- **Established** — directly stated/approved or anchored by an existing finished card.
- **Working** — strongly favored direction that fits established material but can still change.
- **Open** — unresolved possibility; do not build dependent lore as if it were fixed.
